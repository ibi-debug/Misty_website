MISTY LAVERTU
Website Wireframe & Content Blueprint
A page-by-page structure for the Decision-Led Leadership website, built around Decision Drag, The Say YES Movement, and the current offer ladder.
 


Brand hierarchy reflected on the site
• Decision-Led Leadership - category and umbrella positioning
• Decision Drag - core problem
• The Say YES Movement - philosophy and identity
• The Decision Code - primary entry point
• The YES Room - live invitation
• The Say YES Experience - 7-day activation container
• The Standard - signature program
1. Sitemap Overview
Use the homepage as the movement gateway, the Decision Code as the evergreen conversion engine, and campaign pages like The YES Room when they are live.


Global CTA logic
• Evergreen primary CTA: Take The Decision Code
• Seasonal / launch CTA: Step Inside The YES Room
• Mid-tier CTA: Explore The Say YES Experience
• Premium CTA: Explore The Standard
2. Home


Recommended section flow
Section 1: Hero
Headline option: “Decision-Led Leadership” or “Your life changes the moment you decide.” Subhead should position the site for ambitious, high-capacity leaders across business, teams, and life. Primary button: Take The Decision Code. Secondary button: Step Inside The YES Room only when that campaign is live.
Section 2: The Problem: Decision Drag
Define Decision Drag as the hesitation between knowing and moving. Explain that it shows up as overthinking, pausing, delaying, and keeping decisions open too long. Make this feel specific, not motivational.
Section 3: Why It Matters
Show the cost: leaking momentum, weakened self-trust, reduced clarity, inconsistent follow-through, and slower leadership decisions. This is the section that should make the visitor think, “This is exactly what has been happening.”
Section 4: The Shift: Decision-Led Leadership
Introduce the core brand thesis. Explain that life changes the moment you decide and protect that decision. This section should reframe the visitor from waiting for clarity to leading through commitment.
Section 5: The First Step: The Decision Code
Position The Decision Code as the cleanest entry point into the ecosystem. Sample support copy: “Name your Decision State, understand the pattern behind the drag, and see what needs to change.”
Section 6: The Movement
Introduce The Say YES Movement as the philosophy and identity layer of the brand. Clarify that Say YES is not about hype or blind action; it is a way of operating rooted in courage, standards, and self-trust.
Section 7: The Path Forward
Visually show the ecosystem ladder: The Decision Code -> The YES Room -> The Say YES Experience -> The Standard. Keep descriptions short and outcome-focused.
Section 8: About Misty
Use a short authority section focused on decades of lived leadership, not a long biography. Position her as the voice of Decision-Led Leadership and the woman opening a movement, not just selling coaching.
Section 9: Final CTA
Return to the primary action. If no campaign is live, the final CTA should always be Take The Decision Code.
Build notes
• Homepage should feel like a movement-led leadership brand, not a service menu.
• Keep only one dominant evergreen CTA.
• Use campaign CTAs sparingly so the page stays clean.
3. About Misty


Recommended section flow
Section 1: Opening statement
Lead with Misty’s philosophy, not a timeline. Example direction: “I believe life changes the moment you decide.” Immediately connect her personal leadership journey to the work she teaches.
Section 2: Why this work
Explain why Misty built this body of work: years of leading, building, teaching, and watching the same pattern repeat - capable people delayed by hesitation.
Section 3: What she helps people do
State the result clearly: eliminate Decision Drag, strengthen decisive leadership, and move with more standards, self-trust, and momentum.
Section 4: Where this applies
Show that this work applies across small business, entrepreneurship, corporate teams, and network marketing. Keep the language broad so the brand stays bigger than one industry.
Section 5: Authority markers
Include select proof points only if they support leadership credibility: decades of experience, scale of influence, team leadership, rooms led, transformations observed. Keep this concise and premium.
Section 6: Personal note
Add one personal paragraph that makes Misty feel human and grounded. This is where warmth belongs.
Section 7: CTA
Move the visitor back into The Decision Code as the first step.
Build notes
• This page should answer: why Misty, why now, why this work?
• Use story plus authority, not an exhaustive career history.
4. Decision-Led Leadership


Recommended section flow
Section 1: Category statement
Open by naming Decision-Led Leadership as the central category Misty is defining. This should feel like a point of view, not a generic framework page.
Section 2: What it means
Define Decision-Led Leadership as the ability to make clean decisions, protect them, and lead from commitment instead of hesitation.
Section 3: Why most people get stuck
Introduce Decision Drag as the force that keeps capable people in the gap between knowing and moving.
Section 4: Core principles
Use 3-5 short principles such as: clarity follows commitment, open loops are expensive, standards protect momentum, self-trust is built through action.
Section 5: Application lanes
Show how the philosophy applies in personal leadership, entrepreneurship, corporate leadership, and network marketing.
Section 6: CTA
Invite the visitor to start with The Decision Code to see how the philosophy applies to them personally.
5. The Say YES Movement


Recommended section flow
Section 1: Movement statement
Define The Say YES Movement as the philosophy and invitation inside the brand. This is where belonging and identity should be strongest.
Section 2: What Say YES means
Clarify that Say YES does not mean reckless action or forced positivity. It means choosing courage, leadership, and movement when hesitation is no longer serving you.
Section 3: What the movement is built on
Anchor this page in the core values: decisiveness, self-trust, courage, standards, responsibility, transformation, and leadership.
Section 4: Who this movement is for
Speak directly to ambitious, high-capacity leaders who are ready to stop circling and lead with clean decisions.
Section 5: How to step inside
Present the current doors into the movement: The Decision Code, The YES Room, The Say YES Experience, and The Standard.
Section 6: CTA
Invite the visitor to start with The Decision Code or join the current live invitation if active.
Build notes
• This page should feel aspirational, elevated, and emotionally resonant.
• Avoid sounding like a slogan page; keep it rooted in leadership language.
6. The Decision Code


Recommended section flow
Section 1: Hero
Lead with the core hook: they do not have a motivation problem; they have a decision pattern. Keep the page simple and conversion-focused.
Section 2: What The Decision Code is
Describe it as a short diagnostic that reveals the visitor’s Decision State and the pattern behind their Decision Drag.
Section 3: What they will uncover
List the tangible outputs: their Decision State, the pattern slowing them down, and the shift that helps them move.
Section 4: Who it is for
Call out ambitious, high-capacity leaders who feel stuck in hesitation, overthinking, or inconsistent follow-through.
Section 5: Why it matters
Briefly explain the cost of open decisions and delayed movement.
Section 6: CTA block
Repeat the call to start The Decision Code. Remove navigation distractions if possible.
Build notes
• Keep this page focused - no long ecosystem explanations.
• This is the evergreen top-of-funnel page and should be the strongest conversion asset on the site.
7. The YES Room


Recommended section flow
Section 1: Hero invitation
Use the elevated event language Misty drafted. The page should feel like stepping into a room, not signing up for a webinar.
Section 2: What this is
Describe The YES Room as a live masterclass and public invitation into Misty’s philosophy of leadership, courage, and decisive movement.
Section 3: The problem underneath it
Briefly connect the room to Decision Drag and the moment everything changes - the decision.
Section 4: What they will experience
List emotional and strategic outcomes: clarity, a new relationship to decision-making, leadership activation, and movement.
Section 5: Who it is for
Speak to ambitious, high-capacity leaders who are ready for a shift and no longer need more information.
Section 6: About Misty
Use a shorter event-specific bio and tie her authority back to lived leadership over decades.
Section 7: Registration section
Include date, time, Zoom format, and registration CTA.
Build notes
• This page becomes the featured campaign CTA whenever the event is live.
• Use Zoom registration or your preferred event registration system, then route confirmations into reminders and next-step nurture.
8. The Say YES Experience


Recommended section flow
Section 1: Hero
Introduce The Say YES Experience as a seven-day activation container. Keep the subhead focused on Decide. Commit. Move.
Section 2: What this is
Frame it as a transformational experience, not a motivational challenge. Explain that this is where people stop circling and begin acting from leadership.
Section 3: How it works
Outline the simple progression: decide, commit, move. Explain daily rhythm at a high level without overcomplicating the page.
Section 4: What changes in seven days
Focus on outcomes like self-trust, cleaner decisions, increased movement, reduced hesitation, and stronger standards.
Section 5: What they receive
List the components at a high level - workbook, tracker, daily implementation, community, live calls if applicable.
Section 6: Who it is for
Best for people who already know their pattern and are ready to install a new way of operating.
Section 7: CTA
Invite them into the experience or to join the waitlist if the next cohort is not yet open.
9. The Standard


Recommended section flow
Section 1: Hero
Position The Standard as Misty’s signature transformational program. The page should feel premium, clear, and standards-led.
Section 2: What The Standard is
Explain that this is the deeper body of work for people ready to lead, build, and operate at a higher standard.
Section 3: Transformation promise
Focus on identity and leadership transformation rather than quick wins. This is where language like standards, self-trust, decision quality, and leadership embodiment belongs.
Section 4: Who it is for
Call in the person who is ready to move beyond diagnosis and short activation into sustained transformation.
Section 5: Program architecture
Outline the key components only at the right level: curriculum, coaching, implementation, community, or access model once defined.
Section 6: Why now
Speak to the cost of staying in Decision Drag at a higher level of life and business.
Section 7: CTA
Apply, join the waitlist, or request details based on how Misty wants to sell it.
Build notes
• Keep this page premium and clean. It should feel like the deepest level of the work.
10. Resources & Contact
These pages are lighter structurally but still important for trust, discoverability, and business operations.


11. Messaging and UX guardrails
• Do not position the site like a generic coaching site or motivational brand.
• Do not use “meant for more” language.
• Keep Decision-Led Leadership as the umbrella, Decision Drag as the problem, and The Decision Code as the first step.
• Use transformational language over motivational language.
• Make sure all pages speak broadly enough to serve small business owners, entrepreneurs, corporate teams, and network marketing without making the site feel trapped in one industry.
• Maintain one clear evergreen CTA across the site: Take The Decision Code.

12. Suggested build order
• Phase 1: Home, About Misty, The Decision Code, The YES Room, Contact, thank-you pages
• Phase 2: Decision-Led Leadership, The Say YES Movement, Resources
• Phase 3: The Say YES Experience, The Standard, additional nurture and campaign pages
